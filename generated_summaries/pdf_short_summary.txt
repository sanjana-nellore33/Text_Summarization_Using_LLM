Text messaging has sparked a debate over its impact on student writing. Critics, including some educators, argue that textspeak—characterized by abbreviations and a lack of mechanics—ruins spelling and grammar. Conversely, supporters contend that texting enhances conciseness, creativity, confidence, and writing practice, viewing it as a natural language evolution. 

To investigate, author Michaela Cullington surveyed students and teachers, and analyzed college research papers. Her findings, supported by linguistic experts and spelling studies, indicate that students rarely use textspeak and understand it belongs only in informal contexts. Ultimately, evidence suggests texting has no significant negative effect on formal writing abilities.
